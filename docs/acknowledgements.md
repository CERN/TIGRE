# Acknowledgements

TIGRE exists thanks to everyone who has contributed code, reported bugs and
brought new problems to solve over the past decade. Many contributors are
listed in the
[README](https://github.com/CERN/TIGRE#contributors-), and many more have
helped through issues and discussions. The requests of TIGRE's users are
why it handles so many real scanners and geometries.

## Funding

The development and maintenance of TIGRE have been funded by:

- **CERN**, through CERN Knowledge Transfer;
- the **Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council (EPSRC)**, UK,
  grants 1431573, EP/R002495/1, EP/N014588/1 and EP/T003553/1;
- the **European Metrology Research Programme** (EMPIR), grant 17IND08;
- the **Accelerate Programme for Scientific Discovery**, University of
  Cambridge.

## Hardware and facilities

- **NVIDIA** donated GPUs used to develop TIGRE (a Tesla K40c and a
  Titan Xp).
- The **µ-VIS X-ray Imaging Centre**, part of the National Facility for
  laboratory-based X-ray CT (EPSRC EP/T02593X/1), and the **IRIDIS High
  Performance Computing Facility**, both at the University of Southampton,
  provided imaging and computing facilities.

These are the acknowledgements of the three papers about TIGRE
([2016](https://doi.org/10.1088/2057-1976/2/5/055010),
[2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpdc.2020.07.004) and
[2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/2631-8695/adbb3a)), which also thank the
funders of their co-authors and the people who shared data.

The copyright and licence of TIGRE are held by the University of Bath and
CERN; see [Who makes TIGRE](index.md#who-makes-tigre).
